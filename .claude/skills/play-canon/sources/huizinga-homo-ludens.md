## Core concepts

- **Free.** All play is first of all a free activity. Play to order is not play; at
  most it is a commissioned copy of play (pp. 20-21).
- **Apart.** Play is done "as if" and felt to stand outside ordinary life, yet it can
  absorb the player completely. No material interest attaches to it, and no profit is
  made by it (p. 27).
- **The playground.** The stadium, the card table, the magic circle, the temple, the
  stage, the screen, and the court of justice are all play-grounds in form and function:
  temporary worlds within the ordinary one, set apart, where their own rules hold. Once
  played, a game lives on in memory, passes into tradition, and can be repeated at any
  time; repeatability is one of its essential properties (p. 23).
- **Order.** Inside the playground there is an order of its own, and it is absolute.
  Play creates order and is order, bringing a temporary, limited perfection into an
  imperfect world. The smallest deviation spoils the whole game (p. 24).
- **Tension.** Tension means uncertainty and chance: a straining toward resolution, where
  with some effort something has to come off (p. 24).
- **Spoil-sport.** Once the rules are broken, the play-world collapses; the referee's
  whistle breaks the spell and brings back the ordinary world for a moment. The cheat
  pretends to play and, at least in appearance, respects the magic circle. The
  spoil-sport withdraws and shatters the world, exposing how fragile it is, and is
  forgiven less readily than the cheat (p. 25).
- **Play-community.** A team tends to last beyond the game. The feeling of being
  together in an exceptional situation, apart from others, keeps its charm after the game
  ends: the club belongs to play as the hat to the head (p. 26). Play groups set
  themselves apart through secrecy and disguise (p. 27).
- **Contest and representation.** Play is a contest *for* something or a representation
  *of* something, and the two can merge (p. 28).
- **Loss of the play-spirit.** When play becomes over-systematized and regimented, it
  keeps its forms but loses the spirit of play.

## Guidance

### Interactions

- Make entry an invitation the player chooses: a clear first move, never a requirement.
  (Free)
- Mark the threshold with a small ritual that opens and closes the play-space: dealing
  the cards, opening the case, a start sound. (The playground)
- Apply the rules the same way every time inside the play-space. (Order)
- Keep an outcome in doubt, so something can come off or fail. (Tension)
- Give insiders ways to recognize each other: shared signs, private jokes, costume,
  secret knowledge. (Play-community)
- Make every pause and exit deliberate, like a referee's whistle: each one breaks the
  spell for a moment, so place them with care. (Spoil-sport)

### Structures

- Build bounded sessions with a clear start, a duration, and an end, repeatable at will,
  so a good session can become a tradition. (The playground)
- Give the group a life beyond the session: a name, a club, a shared record.
  (Play-community)
- Separate the play-space visually, physically, or in time from ordinary use of the
  thing. (Apart)
- Organize the play as a contest for something, a representation of something, or both,
  and make which one it is obvious. (Contest and representation)

### Content

- Give stakes that matter inside the circle even when they don't outside it: tokens,
  titles, honor. (Apart)
- Supply material for make-believe and secrecy: lore, codes, roles. (Play-community)

### Style

- Let players commit fully. The tone treats the play as apart from ordinary life but
  played in earnest, never mocking the player for caring. (Apart)
- Add festive, ceremonial touches at the thresholds. (The playground)

## Anti-patterns

- **The spoil-sport inside the circle.** Forced sign-ups, ads, chores, or real-world
  demands that interrupt play. (Spoil-sport)
- **Compulsory fun.** Participation required by an institution and labeled play. (Free)
- **Bendable rules.** Rules that give way for the designer's convenience or the business
  model. (Order)
- **A foregone conclusion.** Outcomes decided in advance, so nothing is at stake.
  (Tension)
- **Regimented play.** So much system, scoring, and professional structure that the play
  keeps its forms and loses its spirit. (Loss of the play-spirit)

## Review criterion

- **Set:** Huizinga
- **Source:** *Homo Ludens: A Study of the Play-Element in Culture* (1938), Huizinga
- **Concepts:** Free, Apart, The playground, Order, Tension, Spoil-sport,
  Play-community, Contest and representation, Loss of the play-spirit
- **Criterion:** The play-world holds. Entry is voluntary, its edges are clear, its rules
  hold throughout, and nothing drags the player back into ordinary life mid-play.
- **Question seeds:**
  - From the artifact alone, can a newcomer tell where the play begins and ends?
  - Do the rules hold everywhere inside the play-world, or does [element] break the
    order?
  - Is there a spoil-sport element inside the play-world, such as a forced chore, an
    upsell, or a real-world demand?
- **Misapplied when:** commerce alone is scored as failure. The question is whether
  commerce intrudes into the play-world.
- **Default binding:** yes

  a space.
- **Weak for:** tools with no bounded session; ambient or background experiences.
