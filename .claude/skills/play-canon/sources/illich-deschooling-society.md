## Core concepts

- **Process confused with substance.** Schooling teaches pupils to confuse teaching with
  learning, grade advancement with education, a diploma with competence, and fluency
  with the ability to say something new. Their imagination is schooled to accept service
  in place of value (p. 3). School teaches that instruction produces learning, and its
  existence produces the demand for schooling (p. 18).
- **Measurement.** School initiates the young into a world where everything can be
  measured, though personal growth can't be measured or compared with anyone else's
  achievement (p. 19).
- **Manipulative and convivial institutions.** Institutions sit on a spectrum (p. 24).
  Convivial ones are distinguished by spontaneous use: phone links, subways, mail,
  markets, parks, and sidewalks need no hard or soft sell to be used. Toward the
  manipulative end, the rules increasingly call for unwilling consumption or
  participation (p. 25).
- **Addiction.** Manipulative institutions are socially or psychologically addictive.
  Escalation prescribes more treatment when smaller amounts haven't worked; habituation
  hooks people on needing more of the process or product. Convivial institutions tend to
  limit themselves (p. 25).
- **Learning webs.** A good educational system gives everyone who wants to learn access
  to resources at any time in life, lets those who want to share what they know find
  those who want to learn it, and lets anyone with an issue bring it to the public
  (p. 33). Four networks do this: reference services to educational objects, skill
  exchanges, peer-matching, and reference services to educators-at-large (p. 34).
- **Ritual.** The ceremony or ritual of schooling is itself a hidden curriculum: the
  structure teaches its own lesson, whatever the content says (p. 16).
- **Learning outside instruction.** "Most learning happens casually" (p. 7). "Most
  learning is not the result of instruction. It is rather the result of unhampered
  participation in a meaningful setting" (p. 18).

## Guidance

### Interactions

- Reward the activity itself. Any measure should mirror progress, not become the goal.
  (Process confused with substance, Measurement)
- Let players choose what to learn and when. (Learning webs)
- Help players find each other by shared interest, and let them teach each other.
  (Learning webs: peer-matching, skill exchanges)

### Structures

- Give open access to materials and tools instead of gated sequences. (Learning webs)
- Design convivially: the player uses the thing spontaneously, on their own terms, with
  no hard or soft sell. (Manipulative and convivial institutions)
- Let the design limit itself: when the player has what they came for, it lets them go.
  (Addiction)

### Content

- Build capability that works outside the product, through participation in a
  meaningful setting rather than instruction alone. (Learning outside instruction)

### Style

- Use a plain, non-institutional tone, with no grades dressed up as rewards. (Ritual)

## Anti-patterns

- **Proxy rewards.** Points, streaks, badges, or certificates standing in for play or
  learning. (Process confused with substance, Measurement)
- **Dependence by design.** Engagement loops that escalate (more of the same when it
  isn't working) or habituate (a need for more of the product), so the player can only
  play or learn through it. (Addiction, Manipulative and convivial institutions)
- **Gatekeeping.** Locked sequences and certification where open access would work.
  (Learning webs)
- **A hidden lesson.** The structure teaches compliance or consumption, whatever the
  content says. (Ritual)
