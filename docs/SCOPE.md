# Music Histories · system scope

Recorded 2026-11-16 at the owner's request, from the project brief, recovered
sources, and the hierarchy discussed in chat. This describes the system to build;
it does not approve unpublished copy, visual rules, rights, or publication.

## Hierarchy

**Series → course → edition → chapter → page/activity.** A course owns its film
and listening manifests; each core film anchors one chapter. Listening assignments
connect to chapters and can bridge them. Color and monochrome are formats of an
edition, not separate courses or automatically separate editions.

| Level | Required elements |
|---|---|
| Series | Purpose, adult audience, learning approach, course criteria, connections, shared editorial/visual identity, approved examples, reusable chapter process, continuity and approval rules |
| Course | Stable identity, number/title, cultural/geographic scope, theme, central questions, selection criteria, core-film order, extensions, listening course, workbook introduction and synthesis |
| Edition | Named version of course selections/order/content, source baseline, formats, production specification, current state, predecessor, approvals, release reference |
| Chapter | Primary core film, purpose/question, film introduction, before/during/after viewing invitations, associated listening, evidence/interpretation, reflection, extensions, next-chapter bridge |
| Page/activity | One dominant purpose/action, exact copy/assets, response space, learning rationale, appropriate effort, editorial/visual review, print proof, provenance and sign-off |

Future course titles and scope remain undecided unless established by sources.
Course I is Music, Power & Memory; Amadeus is Chapter 1. Do not create a future
course simply because a chapter has a cross-course bridge.

## Film course

Record core/extension status, selection rationale, film quality criteria,
historical/story anchor, production year, place, relationship to music history,
order, carry question, connections/gaps, availability and date checked.
Keep story setting, musical origin, production, and revival dates distinct.
Do not represent historical analogy as documented influence.

## Listening course

Record the work, artist, preferred performance, selected recording, listening role,
curated link, exact assigned excerpt, time bounds/duration when verified, purpose,
and availability. Preserve preferred recording versus actual curated selection.
Manual overrides outrank automated candidates. Keep Before, Inside, Beside, After,
and Bridge as common roles; preserve legacy labels and flag unresolved mappings.

Listening may reinforce film ideas, investigate music heard in the film, compare
interpretations, examine musical themes, or open cultural/historical context beyond
the film. A recording container (opera, act, album) is not the assigned excerpt.

## Workbook

Provide course introduction/navigation; one chapter per core film; course synthesis
and further exploration. Activities may invite noticing, listening, drawing,
comparing, questioning, making, or reflecting. Not every selection needs its own
activity. Note-taking space is not automatically an exercise.

Apply the Inverse Play Law: demanding viewing/listening receives lower-friction
workbook interaction. Explain why each activity earns its space. Coordinate
activity, illustration, and response space as a learning unit.

## Shared records

1. Film manifest: choices, sequence, historical anchors, rationale, extensions.
2. Listening manifest: recordings, assignments, roles, durations, links.
3. Chapter/page register: purpose, activity, assets/evidence, copy/design/production
   states, concrete deliverable, acceptance criteria, reviewer.
4. Source/rights register: identity, version, hashes, retrieval, approval, provenance,
   credits, permitted uses, publication status. Register new assets as they arise.
5. Decision log: scope, rationale, status, owner/date, evidence, supersession.

The [record contract](../records/README.md) defines storage, references, edition
rules, and the boundary between these records and the work tracker. Missing
information stays explicit; an empty template is not evidence of readiness.

## Production and review

Research/activity brief → exact copy and editorial review → owner copy approval →
composition/prompt tied to approved inputs → exact editable reconstruction →
color/monochrome print-size proof → owner disposition. Early maquettes are
proposals, not approved-copy production. Preserve exact puzzle geometry/key and
use genuine, credited archival facsimiles.

Course I first milestone: proofed Who’s Who page with a research-to-image record.
Validate Mozart’s Hand and The Commendatore Scene next, before full production.

## Completion and exclusions

A complete course edition has coherent reviewed manifests; assigned listening
scopes; reviewed chapters/pages; source and rights evidence; resolved approval
conflicts; working availability links; and accepted print proofs in each claimed
format. Publication requires explicit owner approval of exact content and visibility.

This task creates records and templates. It does not complete the workbook, verify
all historical claims/playback/rights, publish playlists, approve artwork, choose
new courses, or authorize external publication.
