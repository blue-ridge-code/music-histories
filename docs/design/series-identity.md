# Series visual identity — interview and decision record

Status: open; no visual rules approved. Source: S00; S02/S04/S08/S11/S12 pending.
Reviewer and approval authority: project owner.

## First question

With titles covered and the pages printed in grayscale, what should make an
Amadeus page and a page about a very different musical genre unmistakably belong
to Music Histories: **a consistent editorial structure** (type, spacing,
navigation), **a recurring visual voice** (illustration, framing, materials),
or **a recurring way of inviting the learner to notice and respond**?

The options clarify priorities; they do not exclude combining them. Color alone
cannot answer this test. No genre for a future course is assumed.

## Interview loop

Ask this question alone, record the owner's answer, then challenge it with a
concrete cross-genre or page-type counterexample. Research a relevant precedent
after the answer, recording an inspectable source, observed feature, and limits
of transfer. Propose a candidate rule with rationale and a checkable test.
The owner approves, revises, or rejects it before it becomes series authority.
No precedent has been researched or claimed in this first-session inventory.

## Rule record required for each approved choice

ID; status (candidate/approved/rejected/superseded); owner and date; sources;
rationale; series invariant; allowed course/chapter/genre/page variation;
agent instructions; reviewer test; linked approved examples and counterexamples;
supersedes/superseded-by.

Validate each candidate against Who’s Who, Mozart’s Hand, and The Commendatore
Scene. At agreed print size, cover titles and compare color and grayscale.
Distinguish owner recognition judgments from mechanical checks.
No example is approved merely because it was generated or included in a prompt.
