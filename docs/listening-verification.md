### Haydn story: early accounts, not a reconstructed transcript

Scans of the following sources were inspected:

- Georg August Griesinger, Biographische Notizen über Joseph Haydn (Leipzig,
  Breitkopf & Härtel, 1810), printed [page 28](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_sGhDAAAAcAAJ/page/n33/mode/1up)
  and [page 29](https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_sGhDAAAAcAAJ/page/n34/mode/1up).
  He explicitly attributes the account to Haydn, describes an extended stay,
  married musicians wanting to return, the staged departures, and the next-day
  order to leave. He does not print dialogue for the prince.
- Albert Christoph Dies, Biographische Nachrichten von Joseph Haydn (Vienna,
  Camesina, 1810), pp.45–47; [page 47](https://archive.org/details/10600111bsb/page/n52/mode/1up)
  prints a different reported statement by the prince. This is evidence of an
  early published attribution, not an authenticated contemporary quotation.

The accounts differ in staging detail. In particular, Dies's narrative should
not be used to establish the actual final orchestration. The supplied English
prince quotation, a precise instrument departure list and an unison claim are
omitted from the new draft. Its social context is attributed to Griesinger's
later account. Interpretive connections to work and patronage are labeled as
ways to hear the music, not independent historical facts.
