# A5 · Point of view

> Component of the [language persona system](../language.md) — the lens and angle
> through which the writer sees and tells. Kickoff fills "Project setting" when
> voice matters; blank = template defaults.

## What it is

Point of view is who speaks and how much they know: first person ("I"), second
("you"), third ("he/she/they"); omniscient vs limited knowledge; narrative distance
(LitCharts). First person removes the intermediary between reader and subject;
third inserts one.

## Levers

- Person choice: first = intimacy and confession; second = the reader on the
  field; third = freedom, the most common.
- Knowledge frame: omniscient (all-knowing) vs limited (inside one head).
- Distance: deep interiority vs detached observation.
- Consistency: alternating POV is a deliberate device, never drift.
- Project-prose note: most non-fiction project output is second-person instruction
  ("run the script") or first-plural decisions ("we chose") — that choice lives
  here too.

## Template examples

### Put the reader in the action

**Before:** "Users are advised to initiate kickoff before submitting task work."

**After:** "Say \"start the kickoff\" — or just give it a task; `AGENTS.md` Rule #1 makes
kickoff happen first either way."

_Why: Second person places the reader inside the procedure. Source:
[`README.md`](../../../README.md)._

### Use the implied you for agent instructions

**Before:** "It is expected that entries will be limited to choices of lasting
importance."

**After:** "Only log decisions that are **hard to reverse, surprising, or change
scope** — not routine choices."

_Why: The omitted subject is an implied `you`; the instruction speaks directly without
repeating the reader's role. Source: [`docs/decisions.md`](../../decisions.md)._

## Kickoff questions

- Who speaks in this project's output — "I", "we", "you", or an impersonal third?
- For creative work: whose head is the reader in, and how deep?
- Is one POV mandatory per document type?

## Default (minimal kickoff)

Second person for instructions ("run the script"), first-plural for decisions and
statements ("we chose"), third person avoided unless the project is narrative. Minimal
kickoff writes this default into "Project setting" as-is; the full A1–B3 workshop is what
refines it later, and is where creative work sets real narrative POV.
