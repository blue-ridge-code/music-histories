# A1 · Tone

> Component of the [language persona system](../language.md) — the writer's attitude
> toward the topic and the reader (serious, playful, angry…). Kickoff fills
> "Project setting" when voice matters to the project; blank = template defaults.

## What it is

Tone is the attitude expressed in the writing itself. It must suit audience and
purpose, and it may flex per piece while the project's voice stays constant — you
speak differently to a friend and to a crowd, yet remain yourself (Wheaton).
Tone is carried mostly by diction (A2) and syntax (A3); this guide sets the target
they aim at.

## Levers

- **NN/g four dimensions**, each a 1–5 spectrum — funny ↔ serious · formal ↔ casual ·
  respectful ↔ irreverent · enthusiastic ↔ matter-of-fact. A tone profile is a point
  in this 4-D space. Readers reliably notice shifts.
- **Wheaton spectra** — objective ↔ subjective, logical ↔ emotional,
  intimate ↔ distant, serious ↔ humorous.
- Small wording shifts move a dimension ("We apologize for the error" → "Oops!").
- Relation to `docs/TONE.md`: its formality and warmth dials are the coarse setting;
  this guide adds the descriptive layer (tone words, anti-tone words).

## Template examples

### Direct, not ceremonial

**Before:** "It is required that all agents commence by completing the prescribed
orientation process."

**After:** "Agents: run the `kickoff` skill before any other work (AGENTS.md, Rule #1)."

_Why: The imperative is serious, respectful, and matter-of-fact. It assumes a capable
reader. Source: [`docs/PROJECT.md`](../../PROJECT.md)._

### Certain about uncertainty

**Before:** "When verification cannot be obtained, it may be prudent to characterize the
matter accordingly."

**After:** "If you cannot verify something, **say it is unverified** — never guess and
present it as fact."

_Why: The tone stays calm while setting a firm evidence boundary. Source:
[`AGENTS.md`](../../../AGENTS.md)._

## Kickoff questions

- What are the project's 3–5 target tone words (e.g., "calm, direct, dry-humored")?
  And the anti-tone words — what must it never sound like?
- Where does it sit on each NN/g dimension?
- What emotional state is the reader in on arrival (angry, anxious, bored)? NN/g:
  match tone to that state, not to the writer's mood.

## Default (minimal kickoff)

Median point on every NN/g dimension (`3/5`): funny↔serious, formal↔casual,
respectful↔irreverent, enthusiastic↔matter-of-fact all centered. Tone words and
anti-tone words come from the four minimal kickoff questions (see kickoff SKILL.md);
reader arrival state defaults to neutral/attentive. Minimal kickoff writes this default
into "Project setting" as-is; the full A1–B3 workshop is what refines it later.
