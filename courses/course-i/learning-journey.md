# Course I · twenty-week learning journey

**Authoring proposal for owner review.** The owner confirmed ten canonical films
over twenty weeks, highly self-directed participation, typical 5–15-minute
workbook visits, and recall waypoints without tests or rubrics. The detailed
thread map and placements below are proposed, not approved manuscript copy.

Authorities: [learning design](../../docs/learning-design.md),
[film manifest](editions/working/records/films.csv),
[listening manifest](editions/working/records/listening.csv), source IDs S05/S06/S16.
The existing film order is preserved. Weeks are relative to the learner's start;
there are no calendar deadlines or catch-up penalties.

## Four outcomes, three recurring threads

- **O1:** interpret how filmmakers shape our understanding of music in culture.
- **O2:** distinguish audible elements, historical trends and echoes in pop culture.
- **O3:** connect composers, artists, performers and directors with works.
- **O4:** anticipate musical structure by noticing recurrence, contrast and change.

| Thread | Recurring question | How it deepens |
|---|---|---|
| T1 · Music and power | Who is music for, and who gets to decide? | Patronage and work → public entertainment and politics → institutions and cultural authority |
| T2 · Film and cultural memory | What does the film invite us to hear or believe? | Artist stories → historical reconstruction and hindsight → competing portraits of places and musical authority |
| T3 · Hearing expectations | What do I notice, expect, and hear differently on returning? | Plain-language noticing → one supported comparison → prediction and reconsideration using another work |

People and works anchor all three threads. Do not add a fourth memorization task
or a roster to learn by rote. Musical form must follow the actual selection:
recurrence and contrast are starting observations, not a claim that every piece
uses the same structure. Not every chapter needs equal emphasis on every thread.

## A flexible two-week cycle

**First week:** encounter the chapter's Before listening, with an audio-friendly
noticing invitation; watch the canonical film when convenient. Other listening
roles retain their intended position rather than all becoming pre-film homework.

**Second week:** return to music and a short workbook invitation; revisit an
earlier idea, explore an optional film, or simply let the chapter settle. More
listening is welcome without requiring more writing.

Offer a small number of visits, not a required count. Each visit should have one
main purpose, a natural stopping point and an easy return cue. The 5–15-minute
target concerns workbook interaction, not the running time of films or recordings.
Do not advertise a total weekly workload until assigned excerpts are timed.

## Listening and people across the layers

Use the existing six selections per chapter as curation, not six equal workloads
or six obligatory workbook tasks. Verify excerpt boundaries and duration before
allocating space. In the first cycle, L001–L006 are the existing assignments;
L005 remains the Commendatore scene only, even if its URL contains the full opera.

For each new listening move, an author should choose an exact excerpt, model one
audible cue, offer a lighter optional hint on a later encounter and eventually
invite an independent connection. These are not fixed chapter-number levels:
keep support reachable and let an experienced learner skip it.

When a person returns, connect the name to a work, role and concrete scene or
sound. Keep composer, performer, director and fictional character distinct.
Source the credits and any historical connection. Do not imply a pop artist
borrowed from a composer without evidence; an audible analogy can be worthwhile
when explicitly presented as the learner's comparison.

## Waypoints and spaced returns

- **Entry, cycle 1:** an optional private bookmark of a sound, expectation or scene.
  No diagnostic test or rating. Retain enough of the source reference to revisit it.
- **Small returns within the journey:** invite a memory before a useful reminder;
  choose a prior encounter that directly serves the present chapter. These
  replace exposition or another activity, rather than adding review homework.
- **Midpoint, near week 10:** optionally revisit the entry source or compare the
  paired Weimar films. What has acquired another meaning? Notes are optional.
- **Closing, near week 20:** return to an earlier source; connect a person, an
  audible detail and a filmmaking choice. The learner decides what to carry on
  exploring. There is no completion score, rubric or required performance.

These intervals are a practical course proposal, not a proven optimum. A learner
who has taken a break can re-enter through the current film and one optional
reminder; there is no overdue queue to clear.
