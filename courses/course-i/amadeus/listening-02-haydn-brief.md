## What to keep from the background

This is a strong connection between audible structure and music's social world:
an expected ending becomes something else, the performing group diminishes, and
musicians' working conditions become part of the interpretation. It complements
the film's patronage theme and the course's question of who music is for.

For a first encounter, keep one listening focus: notice the transition into the
slow ending and what changes as the texture becomes thinner. Introduce tempo,
texture and expectation through the experience before adding terminology.
Do not require a key-identification exercise, full exit-order inventory or extra
writing while the learner is listening. This is a candidate authoring direction,
not final learner copy or a completed analysis of the chosen audio.

An optional later visit can connect that observation to the court/patron story,
then ask how the historical anecdote changes the listener's interpretation.
Keep the owner's story as a lead for research, not proof of every dramatic detail.
